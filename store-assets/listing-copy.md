## Full description (max 4000 chars)

```
ParkNext connects people who need a parking spot with people who have one
to spare.

FIND A SPOT
Search the map for parking near where you're actually going, see the price
and hours upfront, and book by the hour — for right now or for later.
Spots often have room outside their listed hours too: search 4–6 PM and a
spot open until 5 will still show up, bookable for the part it can cover.

LIST YOUR OWN
Have a driveway, a spare spot, or space you're not using during the day?
List it with photos, your price, and the hours it's free, and start
getting booking requests — with a push notification the moment one comes
in, so you're never watching the app to catch one.

BUILT FOR INDIA
Prices in rupees, an interface in English or Hindi, and search built
around how Indian cities actually work — dense, mixed-use, and short on
parking.

SIMPLE FOR BOTH SIDES
- See exactly when a spot is free before you book it
- Get the owner's or renter's phone number once a booking is confirmed
- Navigate straight to the spot with one tap
- Rate each other after a booking, so the next person knows what to expect

Whether you're circling the block looking for parking or sitting on a spot
that could be earning you money, ParkNext is the fastest way to solve it.
```
(~1040 chars — comfortably under the 4000 limit; expand if you want more detail on any section)

## Notes

- Both descriptions describe features that exist in the app right now —
  partial booking, push notifications, Hindi/English, phone-number
  exchange on confirmation, in-app navigation, ratings. Nothing here is
  aspirational.
- Deliberately doesn't mention payment, since none happens in the app —
  money is arranged directly between the two parties. Don't add language
  implying in-app payment; it'll contradict the Data safety / financial
  features declaration in Step 7.
- "Built for India" section is optional to keep if you'd rather stay
  generic, but it's true (₹ pricing, Hindi support, OSM/Nominatim data)
  and helps differentiate from western competitors in search.
